About the Containment Geometry Lab

This collection explores how large one symmetric convex shape can become while it is translated and rotated inside another.

Each demonstration follows the same thread: tight contacts form a balanced cage, the balancing weights produce certificate data, and that data controls the achievable scale.

Regular polygons · 2D

For regular polygons, the certificate data lives in a plane. The demonstration connects physical contacts to a balanced normal cage and then to a certificate polygon. Its support distance d(φ) gives the displayed containment scale μ(φ)=1/d(φ).

Platonic solids · 3D

In three dimensions, the analogous certificate is matrix-valued and generally cannot be drawn as an ordinary polyhedron. The demonstration therefore presents a fixed-axis slice. At every slider angle it re-optimizes translation and scale; the score curves form an upper envelope d(θ), with μ(θ)=1/d(θ).

Scope

These are explanatory numerical tools, not formal proof artifacts or authority records from the Coxeter Containment program. In particular, the marked Platonic-solid “best” is the numerically sampled best on the selected one-axis slice—not a certified global optimum over all rotations in SO(3).
